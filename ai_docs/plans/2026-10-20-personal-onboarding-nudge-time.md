# Персональное время напоминания о незавершённом онбординге

Status: implemented locally on `feature/onboarding-nudge-analytics`; automated server and PostgreSQL checks passed, live DEV Telegram acceptance and deployment remain outstanding. The user confirmed that reminders may be sent at the observed time even at night. This replaces the fixed 20:00–21:59 Moscow send window in [the earlier nudge plan](2026-10-06-incomplete-onboarding-nudge.md). It applies to the [stage-specific onboarding and practice follow-up sequence](2026-10-20-onboarding-stage-followups.md), not the opted-in `/remind` schedule. The implementation extends the PR #40 outcome ledger with Flyway V17.

## Product rule

- Infer a separate likely visit time for each Telegram user from **their incoming activity**. An initial `/start` is one valid observation: `/start` at 14:35 with no later activity yields the first reminder around 14:35 the next day, after at least 24 hours of inactivity. Never fall back to a shared 20:00 time merely because there is only one observation.
- Include incoming commands/text, voice, photo and inline-button callbacks, including the first `/start` and later responses to reminders. Exclude bot messages, reaction updates, duplicate webhook updates and background/internal events. Treat messages less than 30 minutes apart as one visit; use the visit's first incoming timestamp as its time. All qualifying visits in the last 30 days contribute to the estimate, with no extra weight for a long conversation.
- For one visit, retain its observed minute. For multiple visits, pick the densest circular two-hour time-of-day window and use the **median visit time within that window** as the preferred minute. Unwrap times across midnight before sorting: 23:55 and 00:10 stay together rather than producing a daytime value. For an even number of visits, choose the more recently observed of the two central visit times; resolve equal-density windows toward the most recent visit. If visits are dispersed with no repeated window, the most recent visit wins. Keep the calculation deterministic and versioned. Using the median within a habitual cluster prevents an occasional unusually early or late visit from shifting the reminder.
- Use observed UTC time-of-day because Telegram does not provide the user's time zone; a stable local habit maps to a stable UTC time. Do not impose quiet hours: an observed 03:00 visit may produce a 03:00 nudge. Adapt when new visits arrive, including after a time-zone or daylight-saving change. This is an estimate, not a claim that a one-time `/start` proves a long-term habit.
- Require 24 hours without any incoming activity before sending. `next_due_at` is the first occurrence of the preferred minute on or after `last_inbound_at + 24h`. If the lower bound falls within that same minute, use the lower bound, so seconds on a single `/start` do not postpone the first nudge by another day. Example: `/start` at 14:35:37 can be nudged at 14:35:37 or on the next one-minute scheduler tick tomorrow. New incoming activity postpones the due time.
- If no one responds, use the same preferred time on later days, subject to the per-stage 10-send budget and final message in the linked follow-up plan. Retain at most one claim per user per Moscow calendar day. Keep the live AI-service state check; stage-specific copy and post-result eligibility follow the linked plan. An outbound nudge is not a user visit.
- Run the one-minute scheduler all day. Permit a due send up to two hours late after a short outage; after that, skip that day's slot and try the next personalized slot. Do not replay missed days. Sending still requires a durable claim immediately before the Telegram call; uncertain delivery is not retried on the same day.

## Data and implementation

1. Add a Flyway migration after V16 for `onboarding_nudge_schedule`, keyed by Telegram session ID, with `last_inbound_at`, `preferred_utc_minute`, `next_due_at`, visit count, calculation version and a dirty/recompute marker. Index `next_due_at` for clean rows. Extend `onboarding_nudges` with nullable `scheduled_for`, visit count and timing-policy version. Old claims stay unchanged and are not reclassified.
2. Use the existing deduplicated `interaction_events` inbound records as the recent 30-day source. At the inbound audit boundary, cheaply advance `last_inbound_at` and mark the schedule dirty for a known unfinished-onboarding user. The audit insert occurs before `startAttempt` on the first `/start`, so `startAttempt` must initialize the schedule from that receipt and invite state. Do not require a successful STT/LLM result to count a visit. Avoid synchronous full-history calculation in the webhook.
3. Each scheduler tick first recomputes dirty schedules in bounded batches from indexed inbound events, collapsing 30-minute visits and calculating the next UTC due instant. Seed pre-existing incomplete users lazily from the last 30 days of inbound audit records; if those have expired, use the latest durable onboarding start/received voice as a one-visit fallback. Use only user-originated timestamps for preferred time; bot-side onboarding events must not move it. Store timestamps/derived minutes only, not message text or audio. Expose dirty-backlog and recomputation failures; a dirty schedule is not sent until rechecked.
4. Replace the 20–21 hour guard and all-day candidate scan with a due query on `next_due_at`, plus the 24-hour, latest-attempt and daily-claim checks and the stage eligibility defined in the linked follow-up plan. Revalidate the schedule and AI-service state just before the claim. Serialize a concurrent inbound schedule update and claim through the schedule row or equivalent transaction so a newly arrived message cannot be ignored by a stale due row. Keep Telegram send and outcome accounting outside that database transaction.
5. After a claim, advance `next_due_at` to the following day's preferred slot unless new incoming activity, a stop decision or the stage budget changed it. A failed/unknown Telegram send still consumes that day's claim as today. Preserve the existing delivery outcome and response attribution in PR #40. Add only aggregate timing diagnostics (scheduled vs accepted time, single-visit vs multi-visit sends, missed grace windows) to the protected dashboard/JSON; do not expose individual activity times.
6. The daily opted-in `/remind` settings remain unchanged. `ReminderRunner` can replace its generic copy with a stage-specific practice message in the same claimed daily slot, preventing duplicate notifications.

## Verification and rollout boundary

- Unit tests for one `/start`, one visit with seconds, multiple events in one visit, median with an outlier, an even number of visits, midnight crossing, dispersed times, UTC/local changes, exact 24-hour boundary, night hours, callback/text/voice inputs, and deterministic tie-breaking.
- PostgreSQL tests with Flyway and a disposable database for initial `/start` arriving before `startAttempt`, deduped webhook events, audit retention/fallback, dirty recomputation, due index/query, concurrent activity versus claim, stage change/replaced attempt, one claim per Moscow day and an outage of less/more than two hours. Confirm old V16 analytics and unknown legacy outcomes remain valid.
- Run `:server:test`, `:server:detekt`, and inspect the due-query plan with representative cardinality. In DEV, verify real Telegram acceptance for an account with only `/start`, a later visit shifting its due time, a completed onboarding suppressing the send and a missed slot. Compare observed send time to `scheduled_for`; local tests alone do not prove live delivery.
- Deployment requires its own authorization. The dashboard shows both timing-policy versions in aggregate data; historical sends at 20:00 are not attributed to the personalized policy.

## Local implementation evidence

`V17__onboarding_followup_schedule.sql` creates the durable schedule and inbound triggers; `FollowupTiming.kt` computes visit clusters and median time; `FollowupStore.kt` uses a due-time index and rechecks eligibility under a row lock. `FollowupRunner.kt` sends at that time or substitutes copy at an active `/remind` slot. The old fixed-hour `OnboardingNudgeRunner` remains for legacy tests but is no longer launched by `Application.kt`.
